Proof of Creation

Proof of creation creates evidence. It does not decide ownership.

Proof of creation preserves evidence relevant to authorship and provenance by connecting an exact file to a creator claim and a recorded time. It strengthens an evidence trail; it does not grant copyright or settle a dispute by itself.

By Jason Colapietro (Johnny Suede) · Founder & CEO of Suede Labs AI

Definition

What is proof of creation?

A proof-of-creation record connects a specific digital file with a claimed creator, named contributors, and a recorded point in time. Its purpose is narrow: preserve inspectable provenance before a work is distributed, copied, remixed, or used by an AI system.

That distinction matters because AI increased the speed of creation without increasing ownership certainty. When media can move and change almost instantly, the record closest to the source becomes a valuable part of the evidence.

The Record

What a proof-of-creation record can show

FingerprintA cryptographic fingerprint identifies the exact bytes in a file. Change the file and the fingerprint changes.
TimestampThe registry timestamp records when that fingerprint was submitted to the record.
ClaimA signed creator claim states who submitted the work and can name the contributors attached to it.

Together, these signals can support a chain of provenance: this file, this claim, these contributors, this recorded time. They make the history easier for a person, platform, or agent to inspect.

The Limit

What it cannot prove by itself

A proof-of-creation record does not independently prove human authorship, copyright ownership, originality, consent, or who created the work first. A timestamp proves that a record existed at a time; it does not adjudicate a competing claim.

  • A file fingerprint identifies content, not the person who made it.
  • A wallet signature identifies the account making the claim, not whether the claim is legally correct.
  • A public timestamp records timing, not priority, originality, or ownership.
  • A registry record does not replace contributor agreements, licenses, copyright registration, or legal advice.
Suede Today

What the Suede IP Registry records

The Suede IP Registry records a file fingerprint, a wallet-signed creator claim, contributor information, and a timestamp on a public chain. That creates a durable provenance record for the submitted file.

The registry does not issue or verify licenses and it does not route payments. Permission still depends on valid authorization from the relevant rights holders. Keeping that boundary clear makes the proof more useful, not less.

Evidence Trail

Preserve more than the fingerprint

A proof record is strongest when it sits beside the materials that show how the work was made and who participated. Keep:

  • source files, drafts, session history, and version exports;
  • dated correspondence and project notes;
  • contributor names, roles, and signed split agreements;
  • licenses, releases, and consent records for third-party material; and
  • the original registered file so its fingerprint can be checked later.
Rights Chain

Proof is the first layer, not the whole system

The rights chain has three separate jobs: prove origin, define permission, and route payment. Once the relevant rights holders and applicable rights are identified and terms are agreed, the record can become an input to the next layers. Programmable IP makes agreed permissions and usage rules machine-readable. Defined splits and payment instructions can then support royalty routing. Proof comes first because neither layer should run on an unexamined ownership claim.

Copyright

Proof complements copyright; it does not replace it

Copyright and proof of creation answer different questions. In the United States, copyright generally begins when an eligible original work is fixed in a tangible form, while a provenance record is technical evidence about a file, a claim, and a time. The practical gap is operational: copyright does not automatically carry creator identity, source history, permissions, attribution, or payment instructions into every downstream system. That is why copyright alone falls short as an operational system, and why creators benefit from preserving evidence before distribution.
