Research

Applied AI research and prototypes

The research questions behind Suede AI's work on voice identity, creative provenance and capture hardware, with a clear path from an idea to inspectable evidence.

By Jason Colapietro (Johnny Suede) · Founder & CEO of Suede AI

Three questions, three kinds of evidence

Voice research asks how a person can be associated with a recording. Provenance research asks how a particular file and the claims attached to it can be traced through a workflow. Hardware research asks whether useful evidence can be recorded at capture time. These questions are related, but they need different demonstrations. A voice comparison, a signed file record and a microphone prototype each establish a different part of the story.

Read the status with the claim

A prototype demonstrates an implementation under stated conditions. A live product provides a workflow people can use. When reviewing a research page, look for which of these supports the statement being made, then follow the relevant record or product documentation. The linked registry documentation describes the current registration workflow.

A practical example: one recording, several questions

Imagine a musician records a vocal, edits the take and sends a final mix to a collaborator. The original capture, the edited vocal and the mix are different files. A useful record would connect those versions, identify who supplied them, and keep any permission for the collaborator's use alongside the delivery. An identity signal alone cannot describe the edits; a file fingerprint alone cannot state the agreed use. Research becomes useful when those records can be connected without treating them as interchangeable.

What makes an evaluation useful

A reproducible evaluation should state the input, software or device version, test conditions, expected behavior and observed result. It should include a case where the system ought to reject or flag the input. For a file record, change the bytes and check what happens. For a voice experiment, distinguish a fresh recording from a replay. These are evaluation questions, not published performance results for the projects listed here.

Choose the relevant research page

Start with VoicePrint for the identity question, provenance for file history and claims, and hardware for evidence at the point of capture. Research notes address the information that a consent or model-use record needs to carry. If your immediate goal is to prepare a work for registration, use the IP Registry's current documentation rather than inferring a product procedure from a research proposal.
