VoicePrint

VoicePrint explores the relationship between a voice sample, a person and a record of consent. The public filing reference is USPTO provisional patent application 63/947,120.

By Jason Colapietro (Johnny Suede) · Founder & CEO of Suede AI

The question being studied

A recording can contain a recognizable voice without establishing who supplied the file or what that person agreed to. VoicePrint's research question is how a voice-related signal could be associated with a credential and an explicit action. The important distinction is between a comparison result and the larger workflow that uses it: a similarity score is one observation, while identity, intent and permission each need their own evidence.

Separate enrollment from later use

An evaluation needs to describe how an initial sample is collected and bound to a record, then how a later sample is compared against it. Record the capture conditions, the action being requested and the version of the comparison method. Keep the authorization decision separate from the raw comparison result so the reason for accepting, rejecting or escalating a request can be inspected later.

Test the difficult cases explicitly

Useful test cases include a fresh sample, a replay of an earlier recording, background noise, a different microphone and an input that belongs to another participant. A research report should identify which cases were actually tested, how the decision threshold was selected and what errors occurred. No accuracy percentage or spoof-resistance claim is published on this page; the cases listed here describe what a meaningful evaluation needs to examine.

Connect the result to a specific action

For a consent workflow, the record should identify the exact work, the requested use and the decision presented to the person. Preserve the version of that request with the response. Otherwise, even a correctly matched sample can leave the central question unanswered: what action did the person intend to authorize? This is why the voice question is linked to the separate provenance and consent research pages.

Public record and technical context

The cited provisional application is a filing, not an issued patent or a certification of performance. Authentication design also needs to account for the requirements of its intended environment. NIST's digital identity guidance is a useful reference for understanding the distinction between biometric comparisons and authenticators; it should not be read as endorsement or certification of this research.
